# Source Code Academy (Source Code Academia) and Sahil Adeem: teaching children, parenting, storytelling, seerah

Research date: 16 November 2026. Method: pages were read directly with curl (sourcecode.academy pages; the Acast RSS feed of the Sahil Adeem Podcast, 464 episodes, https://feeds.acast.com/public/shows/sahil-adeem-podcast; news articles). No audio or video was listened to. Everything about the *content* of talks below comes from episode titles, written descriptions and chapter/timestamp lists only. Items marked "search summary only" were not read on the page itself.

Reading note for the writer: a chapter heading such as "Why Children Should Not Start School Before Age Seven" tells us the topic and the direction of the claim, not the argument, evidence, or practical steps. Wherever only a chapter heading exists, that is stated.

Dating note: podcast dates are Acast publish dates. Several episodes are clearly re-uploads or clips of older talks (for example a "Bitcoin, Metaverse" episode dated July 2026), so a publish date is not necessarily the date the talk was given.

## 1. What does Source Code Academy offer for children and for parents? Is there a children's seerah course? What does the adult seerah teaching cover?

### Takeaway
The academy's children's offering is language, Qur'an recitation and STEM (Arabic for ages 4-7 and 8-12, Qira'at/Qaida for under-12s, STEM for 7+, a teen mentorship for 14-19); there is no seerah course for children anywhere in the current catalogue. Seerah is taught only to adults, as one of the subjects in the 12-month Islamic Foundation course, framed analytically ("mission-oriented", leadership, state-building), plus an older 2023 "Seerat un Nabi Course" whose classes are on the podcast as audio.

### Inferences
- A parent of a 4-year-old can get from this academy only Arabic vocabulary classes by Zoom (ages 4-7) and, later, Qur'an recitation. Nothing it sells teaches seerah to a young child; any seerah for the child would have to be delivered by the parent, drawing on Adeem's adult-level talks.
- The adult seerah framing is political and strategic (identity, community, state, leadership, reform). It is not designed as, and should not be mistaken for, age-appropriate story material for a pre-schooler.
- Class structure for small children (30 minutes, 3-6 pupils, five days a week, play methods: pictures, games, songs, role-play) is the one concrete piece of early-years practice the academy publishes.

### Gaps
- No syllabus, reading list or lesson plan for the seerah component could be retrieved (the page's "Click here to view details" content and the 2023 class recordings are not in readable text).
- The teen mentorship curriculum exists as a PDF ("TMP curriculum slides.pdf") linked from the course page; it was not retrieved.
- Whether Sahil Adeem personally teaches any class for under-14s is not stated; the kids' Arabic and Qira'at classes are taught by faculty, and he appears in orientation sessions.
- The page title of the 8-12 course says "Arabic Language for Kids Ages 4-12" while the body says 8-12; the 4-7 course is listed separately. Treat the age split as recent.

## 3. What does Sahil Adeem say about storytelling, introducing the Prophet and seerah to children, curiosity and "why" questions, age stages, screens and media, parental mistakes, and "reading the seerah through Arabian psychology"?

### Takeaway
In writing, only the outline of his positions is available: stories and heroes shape a child's psychology and the wrong stories weaken children; children follow heroes, so love of the Prophet has to be built as the child's hero-attachment; school should not start before seven and language (three languages, Arabic above all) comes first; fear-based, overprotective and comfort-giving parenting and unsupervised phones are the main parental errors; and seerah must be read "through Arabian psychology" rather than one's own cultural lens. How he would actually tell seerah to a small child is not documented in any written source found.
