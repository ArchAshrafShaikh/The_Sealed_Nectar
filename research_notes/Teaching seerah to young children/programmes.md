Research date: 16 November 2026. Pages were read directly with curl unless a bullet is tagged **[search summary only]**. Claims that rest only on a provider's own description are tagged **[marketing]**. Retailer blurbs usually repeat the publisher's copy, so they are treated as marketing too.

## 1. Which events are selected for young children, in what order, and how many?

### Takeaway
Two models exist side by side. Curriculum-style products (Quranic Tarbiyah, IQRA Network, Learning Roots' map puzzles, Mama Teaches Me) walk the life in order but cap the count at roughly 10 to 25 events, and the youngest level usually stops at the Hijrah. Character-led programmes (Muis aLIVE Kids, Noor Kids, Weekend Learning, Miraj, most Goodword books) pick a handful of episodes to illustrate a value and do not attempt chronology until about age 9.

### Cited Findings

**Quranic Tarbiyah, "Sirah & Stories Level 1"** (read from the full free PDF, 3rd edition 2026)
- The book has 25 lessons: 15 on earlier prophets (Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq, Yunus, Zakariyya, 'Isa) and then 10 on the Prophet's life in Makkah — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- The 10 seerah lessons, in strict chronological order, are: L16 The Best Man Is Born; L17 Everybody Trusts Muhammad; L18 Muhammad Meets Jibril; L19 Who Were the First Muslims?; L20 A Kind King Helps the Muslims (Abyssinia); L21 Two Strong Men Accept Islam (Hamzah and 'Umar); L22 The Muslims Live Under Cruel Rules (the boycott); L23 The Year of Sadness; L24 The Prophet Travels to the Heavens; L25 The Muslims Move to Madinah — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- Hard events are kept in, not skipped: L16 tells the death of the father, mother (age 6) and grandfather (age 8); L23 covers the Year of Sadness and Ta'if; L22 lists "the cruel rules made against the Muslims" — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- Each story "highlights one age-appropriate key lesson", so the order is chronological but each event is tied to one character point (for example L17 trustworthiness, L20 "do the right thing even when it feels hard") — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- The product page summarises it as "15 inspiring Qur'anic lessons about selected Prophets" and "10 engaging lessons from the life of the Prophet Muhammad in Makkah" — [Quranic Tarbiyah product page](https://quranictarbiyah.com/all-products/sirah-stories-level-1/)

### Inferences
- The most common count for a first pass is about 20 (Seerah Trail's 20 tags, Seerah Project's 20 events, Goodword's 20-23 story books, IQRA's 25 topics). Quranic Tarbiyah uses 10 because it covers only the Makkan half at age 5-6.
- Battles are largely absent below age 7. Quranic Tarbiyah Level 1 stops at the Hijrah; Muis Kids uses the cave story, family life and daily habits; Miraj uses the Night Journey and a camel. IQRA Network is the exception: it names Hunayn and "confrontations with enemies" for a course it says starts at age 5.
- Singapore's national curriculum postpones chronological seerah until age 9-11 (Tweens: birth, youth, first revelation at 9; Hijrah and Madinah at 10), so its age 5-8 content is deliberately episodic.

### Gaps
- Safar's actual list of seerah lessons per book could not be read (publisher and main UK retailers blocked by Cloudflare).
- The 20 events in The Seerah Project and on The Seerah Trail are not listed on any page I could read.
- Titles of the other three Miraj titles about the Prophet were not captured.
- Weekend Learning Level 2 contents were not read.

## 2. How is a single lesson or story structured?

### Takeaway
The only programme whose lesson anatomy could be verified from the material itself is Quranic Tarbiyah: a two-page illustrated story ending in a one-line lesson and a du'a, then two pages of four short activities. Other providers describe a similar shape (short story, one moral, questions or an activity) but in marketing copy, and digital stories for this age run 2 to 7 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: each lesson is 4 pages. Pages 1-2 are the story in 5 to 7 short illustrated paragraphs. The story ends with speech bubbles from two recurring child characters (Maryam and Musa) stating the one key lesson and a du'a, for example "Muhammad lost both his parents when he was so young, but Allah took care of him at every moment. O Allah, please always take care of us too." — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: pages 3-4 are activities. Lesson 16 has four: (1) trace the name and colour a heart; (2) fill missing letters in the names of the five people who cared for him; (3) partner or class talk, "Who looks after you? What do they do to help you?"; (4) fill in names on a timeline with ages 6 and 8 marked — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: each lesson has 2 to 4 written learning objectives and 3 keywords (some in Arabic). They follow a recall, explain, apply-to-self pattern. Lesson 16: "Name the parents of Prophet Muhammad"; "Explain how Allah took care of Muhammad when he was little"; "Reflect on how Allah cares for us through kind and loving people" — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: a suggested nashid per story is provided by QR code, and each half of the book ends with a recap game (a board game for the prophets, "a sirah map game, enabling children to retell the Prophet's life in Makkah") — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Weekend Learning Level 1**: "Each lesson begins with a coloring page. The coloring time helps students settle down"; then "short paragraphs introduce two or three concepts" because "attention span of children is small"; then an in-class review section "to allow for short break and reinforce learning"; then homework, with more exercises in a separate workbook **[marketing]** — [Islamic Bookstore AU, Level 1](https://islamicbookstore.com.au/weekend-learning-islamic-studies-level-1-revised-and-enlarged-edition/)
- **Miraj Stories**: interactive stories for ages 4-9 are short. Listed lengths run 2 to 7 minutes for most titles (Night Journey 5 min, Al Qaswa 3 min). Each title's page gives the age range, reading time, "what your child will learn" (four bullet points for the Night Journey) and "questions to talk about together afterwards" — [Miraj stories list](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/stories), [Night Journey](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/stories/the-amazing-night-journey)
- **Miraj Stories**: the Night Journey is framed around a question the child already asks, "why do we pray five times a day?", and opens with "Long, long ago, in the land of Makkah, something astonishing happened" — [Night Journey](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/stories/the-amazing-night-journey)
- **Muis aLIVE Kids**: seerah is not a separate lesson. The whole class is one 3-hour weekly session across four strands, in 4 terms a year — [Muis aLIVE](https://www.muis.gov.sg/ie/Programmes/Kids/Kids-aLIVE)
- **Noor Kids Muslim Treehouse**: two one-hour live classes a week (Thursday and Saturday), each week built around two child-voiced questions (for example week 8: "What If My Little Brother or Sister Won't Leave Me Alone?"), with "games, hands-on activities, and friendly challenges" **[marketing]** — [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse)
- **Noor Kids books**: each monthly book is a comic-style story plus "interactive tools like badges and passports" and "discussion questions that inspire reflective learning" **[marketing]** — [Noor Kids help centre](https://help.noorkids.com/support/solutions/articles/13000117711-what-is-the-character-building-program-)
- **Learning Roots "My Prophet Muhammad"**: one continuous story on large double-spread pictures, with "questions at the end of the story for your child to reflect on" **[marketing]** — [Learning Roots](https://www.learningroots.com/products/my-prophet-muhammad)
- **IQRA Network Junior**: "very simple and concise" content "accompanied by review stations and tests"; 25 topics in 25 hours implies about one hour per topic **[marketing]** — [IQRA Network](https://iqranetwork.com/courses/sirah/)
- **Goodword board book**: each of 20 stories ends with "a simple moral or prayer" **[marketing]** — [Goodword](https://www.goodword.net/products/babys-first-prophet-muhammad-stories-board-book)
- **Shakhsiyah halaqah**: a daily oral circle, "child-centred and sometimes child-led", described as "oral, reflective, dialogic and transformative"; one early-years example session on diversity used a story book as the stimulus — [Shakhsiyah halaqah curriculum](https://shakhsiyah.netlify.app/halaqah-early)

### Inferences
- A consistent pattern for ages 4-6: one event, one moral, a closing du'a or question that turns the story towards the child's own life, then something to do with the hands.
- The "hook" is rarely a formal step. Where one exists it is either a settling activity (Weekend Learning's colouring page) or a question the child already has (Miraj, Noor Kids).
- Quranic Tarbiyah is the only one read that ends every story with a du'a in the child's voice.

### Gaps
- Lesson timing in minutes is not stated by Quranic Tarbiyah, Safar, An Nasihah or Muis for the seerah portion.
- The internal structure of a Seerah Project session and of a Safar lesson could not be read.

## 3. What does the child actually do?

### Takeaway
At 4-6 the child mostly listens, colours, traces, talks with a partner and handles physical pieces (puzzles, tags, crafts). Retelling the story from a map or timeline is the main recall device. Written comprehension and tests appear from about 6-7.

### Cited Findings
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: tracing and colouring, completing names, partner talk, filling a timeline, making the Prophet's family tree (L17 objective), singing a nashid, and retelling the Makkan period with a map game — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: several objectives are things to do at home: "Share the message of Islam with my family" (L19), "Show how I can be trustworthy" (L17), "Explain what I can do when I feel scared" (L18), "Discuss how I can be kind to those who are treated unfairly" (L22) — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: teachers are told to "enrich the learning with action, arts and crafts, and hands-on experiences" — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Learning Roots Seerah Trail**: the child assembles a floor map (87cm x 58cm), then places 20 name tags on the matching pictures; the tags are "interchangeable so you are able to assess your child by asking them to put the name of the event next to the correct image"; older children use grid references from the timeline to find places **[marketing, via retailers]** — [ANT Stores](https://www.antstores.com/collections/types/products/the-seerah-trail), [Tadabbur Books](https://tadabburbooks.com/products/seerah-trail-puzzle)
- **Learning Roots Footsteps of Muhammad**: the child builds a 129-piece picture map and follows a map guide through the events in order **[marketing]** — [Learning Roots](https://www.learningroots.com/products/footsteps-of-muhammad-s)
- **Mama Teaches Me**: stories, poems and 5 craft activities across 20 sessions; a colouring-book version of the story exists; a separate colouring book for ages 3-5 is "built around one simple, repeated question: 'Who was Prophet Muhammad?'" **[marketing]** — [Seerah Project](https://www.mamateachesme.com/the-seerah-project/), [Who was Prophet Muhammad?](https://www.mamateachesme.com/mtm-seerah-who-was-prophet-muhammad-salla-llahu-alayhi-wasallam/)
- **Miraj Stories**: the child taps, swipes and makes choices inside the story ("Children tap, swipe and choose their way through each story"); in Al Qaswa "they can also play with the camel along the way"; the app also has colouring, puzzles and audiobooks for off-screen listening **[marketing]** — [Miraj stories list](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/stories), [Al Qaswa](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/stories/al-qaswa), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1222528421)
- **Noor Kids**: children read a comic, collect badges and a "passport", and answer discussion questions; in the live classes they join games and "hands-on challenges" and may be picked to appear live **[marketing]** — [Noor Kids help centre](https://help.noorkids.com/support/solutions/articles/13000117711-what-is-the-character-building-program-), [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse)
- **Weekend Learning Level 1**: colour, listen or read short paragraphs, answer in-class review questions, do homework — [Islamic Bookstore AU, Level 1](https://islamicbookstore.com.au/weekend-learning-islamic-studies-level-1-revised-and-enlarged-edition/)
- **Safar Textbook 1**: "matching exercises, fill-in-the-blanks, and discussion prompts" **[marketing, via retailer]** — [Almanaar](https://almanaar.co.uk/products/safar-islamic-studies-textbook-1)
- **An Nasihah**: a workbook "with differentiated activities using Bloom's Taxonomy" **[marketing, via retailer]** — [Salam Occasions](https://www.salamoccasions.com/products/an-nasihah-islamic-curriculum-workbook-1)
- **IQRA Network Junior**: looks at images and visual material with a live teacher, then "review stations and tests" **[marketing]** — [IQRA Network](https://iqranetwork.com/courses/sirah/)
- **Shakhsiyah**: children talk in a circle; the page lists asking questions, weighing evidence and visits as activities for history content — [Shakhsiyah halaqah curriculum](https://shakhsiyah.netlify.app/halaqah-early)

### Inferences
- Acting out or role play is not named by any provider read, apart from Quranic Tarbiyah's general call for "action". (Depicting the Prophet is avoided across these products, which may explain this; that reason is my inference, not a sourced claim.)
- Map-plus-timeline is the shared memory device across Learning Roots, Quranic Tarbiyah and Mama Teaches Me.

### Gaps
- No source describes what children do in the seerah part of a Muis aLIVE Kids session.
- The five crafts in The Seerah Project are not named publicly.

## 4. How is content staged by age?

### Takeaway
The common staging is: under 5, a first meeting with the Prophet through love, simple qualities and single gentle stories; 5-7, either selected episodes tied to a value or a short chronological run of the Makkan period; 7-9, the whole life in outline; 9 and over, full chronology including Madinah and the battles.

### Cited Findings
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: six levels for ages 5 to 10. Level 1 (5+, "designed for Year 1 children (ages 5-6)") covers Makkah only; Level 2 is 6+; Levels 3-6 (7+ to 10+) are listed as "coming soon" — [Quranic Tarbiyah product page](https://quranictarbiyah.com/all-products/sirah-stories-level-1/), [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Muis aLIVE**: age 5, love of the Prophet and age-appropriate sunnah; age 6, his titles al-Amin and al-Siddiq and the cave story; age 7, his family, daily routine and "best example"; age 8, a brief life story. Tweens then go chronologically: age 9, birth, youth, early spread of Islam and first revelation; age 10, Hijrah and life in Madinah, "review significant events in Sirah"; age 11, the four Rightly Guided Caliphs — [Muis aLIVE](https://www.muis.gov.sg/ie/Programmes/Kids/Kids-aLIVE)
- **Muis aLIVE Kids** puts more weight on Qur'anic literacy than on the other strands **[search summary only]** — [learnislam.sg](https://learnislam.sg/about-alive/)
- **Learning Roots** (its own age labels): The Prophet's Pond, ages 5-9 / recommended 4-7; Miracles of Muhammad, "Age 3-6+"; The Giant Journey (prophets puzzle), 5-7; Footsteps of Muhammad, "Ages 6+", recommended 6-11; My Prophet Muhammad, "Ages 8+" in the header and "Recommended age 7+" in the features (the page contradicts itself); the Khadijah and Aisha biographies, 11+ — [Learning Roots shop by age](https://www.learningroots.com/pages/shop-by-age46), [Footsteps](https://www.learningroots.com/products/footsteps-of-muhammad-s), [My Prophet Muhammad](https://www.learningroots.com/products/my-prophet-muhammad), [The Prophet's Pond](https://www.learningroots.com/products/the-prophets-pond), [Learning Roots home](https://www.learningroots.com/)

- **Noor Kids**: books for 4-9; live Treehouse classes for 7-12 — [Noor Kids about](https://help.noorkids.com/support/solutions/articles/13000116883-about-us), [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse)

- An Nasihah and Quranic Tarbiyah both slice the life chronologically across year levels (a segment per year), while Muis and Weekend Learning spiral: character first, outline next, chronology later.
- Publisher age labels are loose: Learning Roots contradicts itself on one page, and retailer ages for the same Goodword or Learning Roots title differ by up to four years.

### Gaps
- What Quranic Tarbiyah Level 2 covers (presumably Madinah) was not checked.
- Safar's per-year seerah progression is unknown.

## 5. What role do parents or teachers play?

### Takeaway
For ages 4-8 every programme assumes an adult tells or reads the story; none expects the child to learn seerah alone. The clearest instruction is Quranic Tarbiyah's: tell the story from memory with voice, gesture and props, and do not read it aloud from the book.

### Cited Findings
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: "treat this book as a storytelling and teaching resource rather than a text to be read aloud. Tell and show the stories." The five tips are: know the story and tell it without reading; engage the senses; awaken emotions with eye contact and gestures; vary voice, pace and pauses; use visuals and props — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: "its true impact lies in how the lessons are lived and modelled by adults"; the book is "made for" madrasahs, Islamic schools, parents and homeschoolers — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf), [product page](https://quranictarbiyah.com/all-products/sirah-stories-level-1/)
- **Mama Teaches Me**: written by a "former primary school teacher and homeschooling mama"; activities "tried and tested with my own children"; parents follow a guide linking to what 20 other mothers did for each of the 20 sessions during Rabi' al-Awwal **[marketing]** — [Mama Teaches Me](https://www.mamateachesme.com/), [Seerah Project Guide](https://www.mamateachesme.com/the-seerah-project-guide/)
- **Learning Roots**: the parent uses the Seerah Trail tags to "assess your child" **[marketing, via retailer]** — [Tadabbur Books](https://tadabburbooks.com/products/seerah-trail-puzzle)
- **Noor Kids**: books are designed for parents to read with their children **[search summary only]**; the help centre says the stories are crafted to "spark meaningful parent[-child conversations]" (text cut off in my extract) — [Noor Kids](https://noorkids.com/products/noor-kids-character-building-program), [Noor Kids about](https://help.noorkids.com/support/solutions/articles/13000116883-about-us)
- **Noor Kids Treehouse**: classes are led by two named instructors; the parent's role is enrolling and optional replay — [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse)
- **Miraj**: marketed as safe "to go through it all alone" (a parent review), yet each title gives parents the sources (ayahs and hadith) and "questions to talk about together afterwards" — [Miraj home](https://www.mirajstories.com/), [Miraj prophets](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/prophets)
- **Muis aLIVE**: taught by teachers in mosque-based centres in a weekly class — [Muis aLIVE](https://www.muis.gov.sg/ie/Programmes/Kids/Kids-aLIVE)
- **Weekend Learning**: teacher-led weekend-school class; "schools and parents are encouraged to buy a separate workbook" — [Islamic Bookstore AU, Level 1](https://islamicbookstore.com.au/weekend-learning-islamic-studies-level-1-revised-and-enlarged-edition/)
- **Safar**: a teacher's guide with schemes of work for Key Stages 1 and 2 exists **[search summary only]** — [Darussalam](https://darussalam.com/teachers-guide-for-islamic-studies-book-1-learn-about-islam-series)
- **IQRA Network**: live online teacher; the student must be able to "communicate remotely" and pass each level's test before moving on — [IQRA Network](https://iqranetwork.com/courses/sirah/)
- **Goodword**: books pitched for bedtime reading "with parents or alone" **[marketing]** — [Goodword](https://www.goodword.net/products/goodnight-stories-from-the-life-prophet-muhammad)
- **Shakhsiyah**: a teacher facilitates the daily circle and follows up by observing children in free-flow play in the early years — [Shakhsiyah halaqah curriculum](https://shakhsiyah.netlify.app/halaqah-early)

### Inferences
- Home products (Learning Roots, Mama Teaches Me, Goodword, Noor Kids books) make the parent the storyteller and assessor; class products (Muis, Safar, An Nasihah, Weekend Learning, IQRA) make the teacher the storyteller and give parents homework support at most.
- Only Quranic Tarbiyah gives the adult explicit storytelling technique.

### Gaps
- No programme publishes guidance on how long the adult should spend, or on handling children's questions about the deaths and persecution in the story.

## 6. What pedagogy do they claim, and is there evidence beyond marketing?

### Takeaway
Claims cluster around four ideas: stories teach better than rules, pictures and maps aid memory, one moral per story, and love before facts. Independent evidence is thin. Only two programmes have any outside evidence base: Shakhsiyah's dialogic halaqah (a Cambridge PhD, though with pupils aged 10 and over) and Muis aLIVE (a commissioned evaluation reported only in a Muis press release). Everything else rests on the provider's word and customer testimonials.

### Cited Findings

**Claims (all [marketing] unless noted)**
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: "Stories are undeniably one of the most effective tools of education"; stories should be narrated "with passion, love, and creativity"; aims are to strengthen iman, build love for the Prophet and draw "practical lessons"; "creating positive, joyful memories around Qur'anic learning is essential". It grounds this in Qur'an 11:120 and a report from 'Ali ibn al-Husayn that the Prophet's battles were taught like a surah — [Sirah & Stories L1 PDF](https://static.quranictarbiyah.com/file/QuranicTarbiyah/ebooks/Sirah-Stories-B1-1.0-web.pdf)
- **Learning Roots**: picture association, "your child builds an immediate mental association for better long-term memory"; spatial memory through geographic placement; "wonder-led, iman building storytelling" — [Learning Roots Footsteps](https://www.learningroots.com/products/footsteps-of-muhammad-s), [Tadabbur Books](https://tadabburbooks.com/products/seerah-trail-puzzle), [Miracles of Muhammad](https://www.learningroots.com/products/miracles-of-muhammad)
- **Learning Roots**: the whole-life book gives a "framework" so later stories have context — [Learning Roots](https://www.learningroots.com/products/my-prophet-muhammad)
- **Noor Kids**: focuses on "why it matters" over "what to believe"; says the programme combines "Islamic sciences, child psychology, modern educational best practices" and calls it "a proven curriculum" and "evidence-based"; the Treehouse says it is not "memorization of facts, figures, or history" — [Noor Kids help centre](https://help.noorkids.com/support/solutions/articles/13000117711-what-is-the-character-building-program-), [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse)
- **Miraj**: "Interactive learning. Not passive watching"; "Young children remember a story far better than a list of rules"; stories are "reviewed by Islamic scholars", list their ayah and hadith sources, and "never add words to the Prophets that the sources do not give them"; the app "never preaches" — [Miraj home](https://www.mirajstories.com/), [Miraj prophets](https://www.mirajstories.com/library/prophets), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1222528421)
- **Muis aLIVE**: aims to nurture "practicing and confident Muslims, instilled with taqwa and good akhlak"; values-first and thematic — [Muis aLIVE](https://www.muis.gov.sg/ie/Programmes/Kids/Kids-aLIVE)
- **Weekend Learning**: short paragraphs and breaks to fit a short attention span; "keep it simple and enjoyable, yet informative" — [Islamic Bookstore AU, Level 1](https://islamicbookstore.com.au/weekend-learning-islamic-studies-level-1-revised-and-enlarged-edition/)
- **Safar**: "the national curriculum has been thoroughly consulted" to keep content relevant; aims to build knowledge "cumulatively" — [Hani Bookstore](https://hanibookstore.com/products/safar-islamic-studies-textbook-1)
- **An Nasihah**: differentiated activities "using Bloom's Taxonomy" — [Salam Occasions](https://www.salamoccasions.com/products/an-nasihah-islamic-curriculum-workbook-1)
- **Mama Teaches Me**: "The only way for them to truly love him is to learn about him" — [Seerah Project](https://www.mamateachesme.com/the-seerah-project/)
- **Shakhsiyah**: halaqah as dialogic pedagogy; children "consciously choose" Islam through enquiry, questioning and reflection — [Shakhsiyah halaqah curriculum](https://shakhsiyah.netlify.app/halaqah-early)

**Evidence beyond marketing**
- **Shakhsiyah**: Dr Farah Ahmed, who created the curriculum, describes halaqah in the Chartered College of Teaching's journal as "a daily dialogic circle of learning" at the core of a thematic curriculum, and links it to Robin Alexander's dialogic teaching and the Cambridge Primary Review. This is a practitioner-authored article in a professional journal — [Chartered College of Teaching](https://my.chartered.college/impact_article/educating-for-personhood-personalised-character-education-for-young-british-muslims/)
- **Shakhsiyah**: her 2018 Cambridge PhD studied dialogic halaqah in two British Muslim faith schools, but the empirical sessions were with 10-11 and 15-19 year olds, not early years **[search summary only]** — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/278513)
- **Muis aLIVE**: a Muis media release reports an independent study led by Professor Mohamad Johdi bin Salleh (IIUM) of content, teaching approaches and impact. For ages 5-12 it reports "stronger religious values taking root", "more disciplined approach to daily prayers", "increased confidence in expressing their faith" and "consistent practice of Islamic values in daily life". No method, sample or seerah-specific finding is given, and the report comes from the programme owner — [Muis media release, 2025](https://www.muis.gov.sg/resources/media-releases/learnislam-festival-2025-showcases-diverse-pathways-for-islamic-education-in-singapore/)
- **Muis aLIVE**: a separate academic study of one mosque madrasah reported some students losing interest and inadequate evaluation **[search summary only; the paper was not read]** — [UIKA Bogor journal](https://ejournal.uika-bogor.ac.id/index.php/JIEM/article/view/17496)
- **Noor Kids**: the instructor bio calls it "a character-building program researched at Harvard that's reached over 250,000 families" **[marketing]**. The company's own 2011 blog post "Harvard supports the Noor Kids Story" is about the founders' start-up story; search summaries describe it as Harvard Business School sponsorship of the business idea, not an evaluation of outcomes **[search summary only for that detail]**. No published outcome study was found — [Muslim Treehouse](https://www.noorkids.com/pages/muslim-treehouse), [Noor Kids blog](https://noorkids.com/blogs/news/14288771-harvard-supports-the-noor-kids-story)
- **Quranic Tarbiyah**: 15 customer ratings averaging 5.0 on its own product page, 9 from verified buyers; a not-for-profit initiative of Life With Allah that gives the PDF away free — [Quranic Tarbiyah product page](https://quranictarbiyah.com/all-products/sirah-stories-level-1/)
- **Miraj**: 4.6 average from 742 App Store ratings — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1222528421)
- **Parent opinion on older-style books**: on a homeschool forum a parent judged "Golden Seerah for Young Generation" a fit but "writing doesn't seem engaging", and "When the Moon Split" not very engaging for a child **[search summary only]** — [Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/728439-muslim-posters-arabic-for-12yr-seerah-book-recommendations/)

**Other programmes checked (thin results)**
- **Muslim Kids TV** is described as offering Stories of the Prophets and Stories of the Sahaba; no seerah series for 4-8 with episode list was found **[search summary only]** — [SoundVision](https://www.soundvision.com/node/4298)
- **One4Kids / Zaky**: listed series (A Day with Zaky & Friends, Little Explorers, Hurray for Baba Ali) do not include a named seerah series **[search summary only]** — [One4Kids TV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/one4kids-tv/id1291443677)
- **Ali Huda**: a subscription streaming service for Muslim families; no seerah series for this age was identified **[search summary only]** — [Urdu Mom review](https://urdumom.com/2017/09/netflix-for-muslim-kids-ali-huda/)
- **Goodword "Seerah Activity Book for Kids"** (age 6+): "illustrated stories, interactive timelines, analytical tasks, and creative projects" **[marketing, via retailer]** — [IQRA.org bookstore](https://www.iqra.org/collections/age-6/products/seerah-activity-book-for-kids)

### Inferences
- "Evidence-based" in this market usually means "informed by general education ideas", not "tested". No provider publishes a study of what children aged 4-8 remember or how their attachment to the Prophet changes after its seerah material.
- The strongest design evidence available is indirect: providers who give away their full material (Quranic Tarbiyah) can be judged on the material itself, and it shows a coherent recall, explain, apply structure.
- Shakhsiyah's dialogic approach has the best scholarly backing but the least public detail on seerah content for young children, and its research did not sample this age group.

### Gaps
- No independent, published evaluation of any of these programmes' seerah teaching for ages 4-8 was found.
- No independent parent or teacher blog review of Learning Roots, Safar, Noor Kids, Miraj, The Seerah Project or Quranic Tarbiyah was located and read; reviews found were on providers' own pages or app stores.
- The IIUM evaluation of aLIVE was not found as a published document.
- Muis's own description of aLIVE pedagogy (the "Learning Approach" page) now redirects to the general programme page, so the detailed pedagogy statement could not be read.
- Muslim Kids TV, One4Kids/Zaky and Ali Huda catalogues were not read directly.
