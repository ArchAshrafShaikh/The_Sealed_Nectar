## The goal

The owner has young children who are glued to YouTube. He wants **a YouTube channel that teaches the seerah (the life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ) to children without them noticing they are being taught**: something they enjoy the way they enjoy YouTube, and that benefits them. The source book is *The Sealed Nectar* (Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum).

Alongside it, he was advised to set up **YouTube Kids in "Approved content only" mode** himself, so his children see only videos he has hand-picked. That needs no building.

## The show

### Episode format, about four minutes

1. **Today (about 40 seconds).** A child-sized problem in modern daily life, in the manner of Omar & Hana.
2. **"That reminds me of something that really happened" (about 2 minutes).** Abba tells the seerah story on the swing. The story is shown around one thing from it that is animated: the camel, the scales, the cloth. The thing is shown while Abba tells what happened; it never speaks.
3. **Back to today (about 30 seconds).** The child acts on it, one line states the lesson, and there is a hint of the next episode.

The lesson is said by a character in passing, never by a narrator explaining a moral.

### The setting

Abba and Dadi's home: a garden terrace on top of a high-rise in a busy city. The cousins are together there most days. The lobby and lifts are used when the children need to meet other people.

**`show/locations.md` is the location sheet**: ten locations with a prompt each, the shared style block, the fixed colours, what never moves, and the order to make them in. It was simplified from photos of the real home (not saved).

The storytelling spot is a garden swing bench: yellow frame, curved tan canopy, teal slatted seat, green rings, a red rug over the back. Every seerah story begins there. The night sky and the sunrise seen from the terrace are how the show moves from today into the seerah scenes.

### Visual direction

- Flat 2D picture-book illustration, soft rounded shapes, clean and uncluttered. Not 3D, not photorealistic.
- The show's three colours are yellow, teal and green, with the red rug as the one accent. Seerah scenes use warm sand and gold, with deep blue nights.
- Characters: big heads, large expressive eyes, very simple bodies.
- Calm pace: hold each shot five to eight seconds, with slow camera movement.
- Thumbnails: one character's face large, one object from the story, a plain bright background, no words.

## Rules that must always hold

- **Nobody from the seerah is ever shown.** Not the Prophet ﷺ, his family, companions, or anyone else in the events. No figures, faces, hands, shadows or silhouettes of them. Seerah scenes show places, animals and objects only, shot from where he stands, so things respond to someone out of view.
- **The present-day family is drawn.** The owner decided this on 21 November 2026.
- **Never generate Arabic writing or calligraphy.** Image models garble it. Frames on the walls show geometric patterns with no writing.
- **No music.** Voice and natural sound only.
- **Fidelity to sources.** Retell in new words; do not copy the book's text; do not invent events or dialogue. Each story lists its source.
- **Honorifics** throughout (ﷺ, "may Allah be pleased with him/her").
- Battles and harder events are left out for now; deaths in his childhood are told briefly and gently.
- **Ask before spending his Higgsfield credits**, and show one example before producing many.

### Checking a location picture

- It looks like a storybook illustration, not a photo or a 3D render.
- No people or animals in it; locations are drawn empty.
- No writing, letters or symbols anywhere.
- For the swing corner: yellow frame, curved canopy, teal seat, green rings and red rug are all present, with clear space on the seat and on the grass in front for the family.

### Checking any picture or clip with seerah content

Reject and regenerate if it shows a person, a hand, a human shadow or any writing. The shot where something is "comforted by someone out of frame" is the one most likely to come back with a hand in it.

### Assembling an episode

1. Voices: the storyteller audio is made with `tools/make_voice.py` (see below). Voices for the family characters have not been decided.
2. Cut the clips to the voice so each shot matches what is being said. `imageio-ffmpeg` provides ffmpeg.
3. Strip any audio the video model added. Add natural sound only.
4. Deliver the finished file to the owner; he uploads it.

## Publishing: what the research found

- **AI-made children's videos are under scrutiny on YouTube.** In April 2026 about 200 organisations asked YouTube to ban them from YouTube Kids. YouTube says YouTube Kids limits AI-generated content to a small set of high-quality channels, so a new channel is unlikely to appear there. Third-party guides report that mass-produced synthetic content is now a spam violation; this was not confirmed against YouTube's own pages.
- **Advice given to the owner:** start unlisted, for his own children; make few episodes that are clearly hand-shaped and different from one another; read YouTube's current policies on made-for-kids and AI content before going public; and have someone knowledgeable review each script before it is published.
- Existing channels: IQRA Cartoon already tells the life in order across about 55 episodes; Omar & Hana (about 4 million subscribers) teaches values through a family's daily life. The gap this show aims at is a child's everyday problem answered by a seerah story.
- He has not yet said whether the channel will be public or unlisted, or which language comes first.
